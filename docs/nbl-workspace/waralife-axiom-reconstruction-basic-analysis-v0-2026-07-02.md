## Central Reading

`わらいふ` should not be read as just a self-monitoring app.

It is better understood as a training-assisted assessment loop:

1. the person records life / body-mind / environment fluctuations;
2. the supporter and person review the record together;
3. the supporter learns to separate observation from interpretation;
4. the record is translated into work, environment, health-time, support, and regional coordination questions;
5. the next support action is reviewed through human discussion rather than app advice.

This is highly compatible with Axiom/FCHMA, but the current materials stop mostly at the threshold of the loop. They explain monitoring, reflection, and assessment skill, but the app itself does not yet visibly carry the full Axiom chain:

`evidence -> ICF contact point -> structural hypothesis -> intervention hypothesis -> implementation actor -> outcome -> review / learning`.

## What Is Strong

### 1. The problem setting is real

The materials focus on people whose conditions fluctuate and whose work participation cannot be understood from a one-time interview or diagnosis label. This directly matches the Falcon finding that health time, recovery, work density, disclosure, support, and evaluation must be read as interacting work-design variables.

### 2. The app has a good minimum evidence shape

The 27 items in the start book are grouped into:

- daily actions;
- body and emotional state;
- surrounding situation.

This is a practical entry format for non-specialist users. It can be mapped into ICF/FCHMA without forcing users to speak in expert language.

### 3. The training contains the missing professional layer

The lecture and exercise materials repeatedly emphasize:

- person-centered assessment;
- assessment as collaboration, not sorting;
- information and interpretation separation;
- not treating readiness as a hurdle;
- supporter reflection;
- person / family / supporter / workplace / region fit;
- medical, life, and work coordination;
- multi-agency case discussion.

This is much closer to Axiom than a generic app manual would be.

### 4. The research outputs already contain a skill taxonomy

The assessment skill categories are usable as a first bridge to Axiom:

- A micro: person and family view;
- B meso: supporter and workplace view;
- C macro: region and municipality view;
- D/E/F: fit across person-family, person-supporter/workplace, person-region;
- methods: deepening from hopes and concerns, communication style, observation, existing tools, stakeholder information, supporter reflection.

### 5. The evaluation design has a starting point

The materials include pre/post self-evaluation for GIO/SBO and skill categories, and start-book feedback from users/supporters. This gives a baseline for a stronger validation stack.

## Main Gaps Before Axiom Reconstruction

### 1. App data is not yet a case mechanism

The app records state changes, but the materials do not yet show a typed path from record to:

- evidence object;
- ICF factor;
- relation;
- competing hypothesis;
- selected support action;
- outcome and side effect.

Without this, Axiom can only say "monitoring helped reflection"; it cannot yet validate work-design mechanisms.

### 2. The app asks for state but not enough context of the work contact point

Current items capture sleep, health, emotion, communication, work rules, task, environment, rest, etc. The next version should also capture the concrete contact point:

- what task or transition was happening;
- time of day / before-after relation;
- workload or pace;
- people involved;
- physical/sensory setting;
- treatment, medication, or recovery timing;
- what was tried;
- what changed after review.

This does not need to be heavy. It can be a lightweight "what was happening around this change?" layer.

### 3. Supporter action is under-instrumented

The current app is strongest for person-side monitoring and supporter reflection. Axiom reconstruction needs the supporter/workplace layer to be recorded:

- what the supporter inferred;
- what the person confirmed or corrected;
- what counter-hypothesis remained;
- who needs to act;
- what workplace, medical, family, or regional coordination was requested;
- when to review.

### 4. Evaluation is still mostly confidence / satisfaction / early self-report

Useful, but insufficient for Axiom validation.

Needed next metrics:

- record continuation and dropout pattern;
- review meeting completion;
- observation/inference separation quality;
- person-confirmed useful insight;
- support action selected;
- implementation actor clarity;
- follow-up completion;
- observed effect, null effect, adverse effect;
- person agency / self-advocacy change;
- supporter correction rate.

### 5. Risk of accidental individualization

If rebuilt poorly, the app may imply:

- "manage yourself better";
- "record more and the answer will appear";
- "fluctuation is mainly personal self-care";
- "the app can advise what to do".

Axiom reconstruction must instead keep the core claim:

`fluctuation becomes usable only when it is translated into work, support, environment, health-time, and institution conditions through human review`.

## Axiom Fit

### Evidence Layer

The PDFs are current `evidence_layer` / `source_readiness` materials, not reviewed Axiom core knowledge by themselves.

Safe use:

- source-family inventory;
- assessment-skill taxonomy input;
- scenario fixture design;
- training redesign input;
- app v2 requirements input.

Not safe use:

- public proof of effectiveness;
- legal/current-policy final statement;
- support validity claim;
- automatic advice rule;
- candidate pattern promotion.

### FCHMA Mapping

Current app fields can map as follows:

- `daily actions`: activities, participation, time rhythm, daily living, work routine;
- `body and emotional state`: body functions, mental functions, health-time, personal experience;
- `surrounding situation`: environmental factors, work design, family, workplace climate, support availability;
- `reason for working`: participation meaning, values, future orientation;
- `weekly reflection`: review loop and hypothesis generation entry;
- `supporter joint review`: human review and implementation actor bridge.

### Axiom Kernel Candidate Shape

Each useful `わらいふ` record should become a small kernel candidate, not an advice output:

```text
observation:
  On these dates, this item changed.

source lens:
  person self-record / supporter review / workplace information / medical or family context if available

possible ICF contact point:
  activity / participation / body function / environment / support / time / institution

working hypothesis:
  A condition-window and work-contact-point relation may be opening or closing participation freedom.

counter-hypothesis:
  The change may instead reflect unrelated life events, recording burden, mood, app novelty, or missing workplace context.

next question:
  What was happening in the job/environment/support/time/institution around the change?

review:
  person confirms, revises, or rejects the interpretation.
```

## Real-World Need Readout

The local materials point to five practical needs.

1. High-quality assessment for employment choice and continuation  
The training aligns with the policy direction that assessment should support better choice, not sorting or gatekeeping. Before public or business use, current official details must be rechecked live.

2. Fluctuating conditions need health-time translation  
For mental disabilities, chronic illness, intractable diseases, medication, fatigue, pain, sleep, commuting, and recovery, one-time interviews are too thin. The app can supply time-series evidence, but only if linked to work contact points.

3. Supporters need to learn not to collapse information into judgment  
The lecture material's "information vs interpretation" distinction is a major asset. It should become a visible app/training control.

4. Workplace and medical coordination need shared language  
The materials repeatedly show that medical opinions, workplace requirements, and support plans have different source lenses. Axiom should preserve those differences, not average them.

5. Regional variation is a design issue  
The regional lecture correctly treats resources, transportation, service density, and local relationships as part of the support structure. App v2 should be able to express "this issue is not only the person's condition; it is also a regional coordination condition."

## Reconstruction Direction

### App v2 Direction

Rebuild `わらいふ` as a "shared work-condition reflection app", not an advice app.

Minimum v2 modules:

1. Person record  
Daily action / body-mind / surrounding situation, with optional "what was happening?" context.

2. Reflection view  
7/14/28-day trends, but with prompts that ask about work contact points, not generic self-management.

3. Joint review  
A supporter and person separate observation, interpretation, counter-hypothesis, and next question.

4. Support action log  
Record one small adjustment or coordination action, actor, date, and review window.

5. Outcome review  
Record whether the action helped, did not help, created a side effect, or needs rethinking.

6. Consent and sharing  
Person controls what is shared with supporter, workplace, medical actor, family, or region-side coordinator.

AI may assist by generating candidate questions and hypotheses. It must not produce final advice, final support decisions, or final work capacity judgments.

### Training v2 Direction

Rebuild training around the loop:

```text
record -> jointly read -> separate observation/inference -> generate hypotheses/counter-hypotheses -> choose a small support action -> review outcome -> update assessment
```

Recommended training modules:

1. Foundation  
Person-centered assessment, social model, employment support purpose, current policy context.

2. Reading fluctuations  
How to read health-time, recovery, daily living, work task, and environment interactions from app records.

3. Information vs interpretation lab  
Use app-like records and teach supporters to mark observation, inference, missing context, and counter-hypothesis.

4. Case conference lab  
Bring person, supporter, workplace, medical/family/regional information into one map without collapsing source lenses.

5. Work-design action lab  
Translate the reading into one support action with owner, precondition, review date, and risk note.

6. Reflection and field assignment  
After training, participants test one micro-change in their organization and report what changed, what did not, and what was learned.

Each fixture should test whether Axiom can:

- preserve source lenses;
- avoid diagnosis-first advice;
- ask useful missing-context questions;
- produce candidate hypotheses and counter-hypotheses;
- name implementation actor conditions.

### L3 Training/App Prototype Evaluation

Prototype the joint-review flow without full app rebuild:

- paper or static UI mock;
- supporter marks observation/inference;
- person confirms or corrects;
- one support action is selected;
- one follow-up review is recorded.

### L4 Field Pilot

Only after L0-L3:

- small number of organizations;
- no public claim of efficacy;
- consent and data minimization;
- measure app use, review quality, action clarity, follow-up, and person agency.

Pros: fastest, low risk.  
Cons: app/training reconstruction remains abstract.

### Robust

Build a full Axiom intake pipeline for `わらいふ`, including typed fixtures, review UI, app v2 requirements, and training v2 curriculum.

Pros: strongest foundation.  
Cons: risks widening into schema/runtime/product-scope decisions too early.

### Compromise

Run a 6-8 week Falcon Lab validation sprint:

1. source manifest;
2. evidence-object map;
3. 3 scenario fixtures;
4. joint-review paper/static prototype;
5. training v2 outline;
6. app v2 MVP requirements;
7. Founder/Pro review packet.

Recommendation: compromise.

Reason: It moves from analysis to testable product shape without committing to app implementation, DB schema, public claims, or runtime changes.
