# External Signal Digest - 2026-03-25

## Signals By Track

### Employers
- Observation: On March 5, 2026, the EEOC sued Schneider National, alleging the company withdrew a conditional job offer after an applicant with PTSD requested to bring a service dog to work. This is a direct accommodation dispute, not a general hiring anecdote.
- Inference: Employer failure often shows up at the point where a specific accommodation collides with an employer's default process or risk perception, especially in safety-sensitive roles.
- Normative: Accommodation requests should be assessed against essential job functions and individualized context, not diagnosis stigma or a blanket proof demand.
- Recommendation: If NBL has employer-facing guidance, add an explicit service-animal / accommodation escalation path and make clear that a conditional offer is not a diagnostic veto point.

- Observation: Meta published a February 9, 2026 post describing AI glasses work with the Oscar Mike Foundation and veterans with memory loss/TBI to improve memory assistance and spatial awareness.
- Inference: Major tech firms still see accessibility as a product surface worth co-designing with disabled communities, not just a compliance layer.
- Normative: Accessibility claims should be grounded in lived experience and tested with the people affected.
- Recommendation: Treat this as a secondary market signal that co-design with disabled users is still a live expectation.

### Disabled People And People With Chronic Illness
- Observation: CDC's March 9, 2026 Long COVID page says Long COVID can result in chronic conditions, require comprehensive care, and sometimes cause disability; it can last months to years, and there are no approved tests or treatments that can by themselves confirm the condition.
- Inference: Verification burden remains a major barrier for people with chronic, fluctuating, or hard-to-measure conditions.
- Normative: Support decisions should not require lab proof as the sole legitimacy test.
- Recommendation: Screen NBL copy and intake flows for diagnosis-only or "prove it" language.

- Observation: CDC's clinical guidance says objective laboratory or imaging findings should not be used as the only measure of a patient's well-being, and treatment should focus on specific symptoms or conditions with shared decision-making.
- Inference: The official posture is moving toward function-first, symptom-specific support rather than static diagnosis-first gatekeeping.
- Normative: Functional impact and reported experience matter when evidence is incomplete.
- Recommendation: Use this guidance to sanity-check any NBL framing around illness verification, work capacity, or support needs.

### Supporters And Support Organizations
- Observation: NOD held a March 24, 2026 virtual career fair for disability-inclusive employers and candidates. Its description says employers received training on inclusive hiring, accommodations, and workplace accessibility, while students got coaching on resumes, interviewing, and self-advocacy.
- Inference: The employer side of disability inclusion still needs structured training and matching infrastructure; demand is not self-executing.
- Normative: Disability support should translate into concrete process support, not just awareness content.
- Recommendation: If NBL needs employer validation, NOD is a useful partner for reviewing language and workflow assumptions.

- Observation: Disability Belongs co-hosted the 2026 Disability Policy Seminar and staged a March 24 Hill Day focused on Medicaid, HCBS, education, employment, and economic security.
- Inference: Policy and service organizations are still bundling advocacy, service access, and employment as one connected system.
- Normative: Employment signals should be interpreted alongside benefits, care access, and policy constraints.
- Recommendation: Use this as a reminder that any NBL employer or candidate workflow may need policy context, not just UX polish.

### Research Or Policy
- Observation: CDC's March 9, 2026 Long COVID clinical guidance says primary care providers can manage Long COVID using patient-centered approaches, should not rely only on objective lab or imaging findings, and should set achievable goals through shared decision-making.
- Inference: Official guidance is explicitly rejecting diagnosis-only validation as a complete care model.
- Normative: Care and support frameworks should preserve uncertainty without dismissing the person.
- Recommendation: Prefer function, impact, and support-needs framing over diagnosis verification framing.

- Observation: NIOSH's February 17, 2026 Total Worker Health bulletin says job-related health hazards can worsen pre-existing chronic diseases and employers should consider workplace-based approaches to support workers with chronic conditions.
- Inference: Workplace design remains a meaningful upstream lever for chronic disease burden, not merely a downstream accommodation issue.
- Normative: Work design and health protection belong in the same conversation.
- Recommendation: If NBL touches employer guidance, include workplace hazard and workload context in addition to accommodation text.

## What Seems Structural
- Fluctuating or hard-to-measure conditions keep running into institutions that want static proof, while the lived reality is dynamic.
- Employer failures still cluster around accommodation process design and risk perception, not just intent.
- Disability organizations are pairing direct employer training with policy advocacy, which suggests the field still needs both operational and legislative levers.
- Accessibility is increasingly framed as product and workforce infrastructure rather than charity.

## What Remains Partial Or Risky
- The Schneider case is an allegation, not a judgment.
- The Meta accessibility story is a product/co-design signal, not evidence of a broad labor-market shift.
- The NOD and Disability Belongs events are planned activities, not outcome data.
- CDC and NIOSH guidance are broad public-health signals; they do not specify NBL's exact workflow.
- Missing person, job, environment, support, time, institution, and evidence context remain partial until validated in a concrete use case.

## Next Validation Move
- Audit NBL employer-facing language for diagnosis-only proof standards, then test it against one concrete accommodation example such as service animals or fluctuating fatigue/cognitive symptoms.
- If possible, ask one employer contact and one disability organization to review the wording before it ships.

## Founder Boundary
- Founder action is only needed if NBL is making claims about disability verification, accommodation eligibility, or Long COVID or chronic-illness screening.
- Otherwise, keep this as a monitoring signal and validate before changing product or policy.
